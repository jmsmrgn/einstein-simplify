# Example: a dense source split into parts

**Source** (the operator's summary, reproduced verbatim):

> Entanglement is non-separability relative to a chosen partition or pair of commuting algebras. For pure bipartite states it means Schmidt rank above one, and the marginal entropy is the unique asymptotic LOCC measure (distillable entanglement equals cost, with Nielsen majorization governing single-copy conversion). Mixed states are far worse. Separability membership is NP-hard, and PPT is sufficient only for two-qubit, qubit-qutrit, and one-versus-N-mode Gaussian cases. The measures are mutually inequivalent, and PPT bound entanglement (nonzero cost, zero distillable) makes manipulation irreversible, with NPT bound entanglement still open. Multipartite structure has GHZ and W classes at three qubits, a continuum of SLOCC orbits from four, and monogamy constraints (Coffman-Kundu-Wootters). For identical particles, superselection rules, and gauge constraints, the subsystem itself must be defined through its accessible algebra.
>
> Entanglement is necessary but not sufficient for Bell nonlocality. The hierarchy of entangled, steerable, and Bell-nonlocal states is strict, with Werner's local model, Barrett's POVM extension, and Popescu's hidden nonlocality marking its edges. Bell's theorem constrains parameter independence, outcome independence, measurement independence, and unique outcomes. Quantum mechanics violates outcome independence. Bohmian mechanics violates parameter independence ontically, GRW violates outcome independence, superdeterminism denies measurement independence, and Everett denies unique outcomes. Wood and Spekkens showed that any classical causal account needs fine-tuning to preserve no-signalling. Quantum correlations reach 2√2 for CHSH against the no-signalling value of 4, and MIP* = RE separates the tensor-product closure from the commuting-operator set, a negative resolution of Tsirelson's problem. Loophole-free tests closed detection and locality in 2015, and cosmic-source settings have since pushed choice events back billions of years. Preferred-frame superluminal influence is bounded below by four orders of magnitude above c. No-signalling is the no-communication theorem, which is microcausality in field theory, and joint statistics are Lorentz invariant even though the collapse ordering is not.
>
> Decoherence is entanglement with an environment that records pointer-basis information (einselection), which yields improper mixtures without solving the outcome problem. Gapped ground states obey area laws (proven in 1D by Hastings, open above), and critical 1+1D states scale logarithmically with the central charge. Topological order adds a universal constant fixed by total quantum dimension, and chaotic eigenstates obey volume laws sub-Page under ETH. Entanglement is not sufficient for computational advantage, since stabilizer circuits are maximally entangling and efficiently simulable. In QFT, local algebras are type III_1 and the vacuum is cyclic and separating (Reeh-Schlieder). Entanglement entropy is UV divergent, and only mutual information, relative entropy, and modular Hamiltonians are regulator independent, which makes the Unruh effect the boost modular flow (Bisognano-Wichmann). Holographically, HRT with quantum extremal surfaces sets entropy by extremal area plus bulk entropy, with ER=EPR and entanglement wedge reconstruction as consequences. Island and replica-wormhole calculations recover a unitary Page curve, while gauge constraints and edge modes leave the factorization problem for gravitational subsystems unresolved.
>
> Experiments produce GHZ states of tens of qubits in ions, Rydberg arrays, and superconducting circuits. They also entangle atomic ensembles of order 10^12 atoms and picogram mechanical resonators, and distribute photon pairs over more than 1,200 km by satellite. ATLAS and CMS observed top-quark spin entanglement above five sigma in 2024, a tomographic witness, not a Bell test, because the analyzer settings are not freely chosen. Device-independent QKD, secured by Bell violation and monogamy, was realized in 2022 with ions, atoms, and photons, and squeezed vacuum is deployed in gravitational-wave detectors. Gravity-mediated entanglement proposals (Bose et al., Marletto-Vedral) remain unrealized with a debated interpretive scope, as does whether spacetime emerges from entanglement.

**Invocation:** `/einstein-simplify for a high school physics class of very capable students encountering this for the first time`. **Purpose:** understand. **Medium:** a handout the class keeps.

**Takeaway:** two particles can be linked so that neither one has its own answer, the link appears only when the results are compared by ordinary means, and it cannot carry a message on its own.

**New ideas (3 per part):**

- **Part 1:** one joint description that will not split into two; a direction chosen for each measurement; random results whose comparison is not random.
- **Part 2:** pre-set answers cap the number at 2; the experiment breaks the cap; the conclusion is conditional, and no message travels either way.
- **Part 3:** any leak of information destroys the sharp link; the link is a resource for computers and secret keys; one outcome per run stays unexplained.

**Output** (the handout, ready to print):

> ## Entanglement: a first look
>
> ### 1. Two things, one description
>
> Picture a pair of gloves. You put the left glove in one box and the right glove in another, then mail the boxes to opposite ends of the country. You open yours and find the left glove. Instantly, without anything travelling between you and your partner, you know the other box holds a right glove. Nothing strange is going on there. The gloves were matched when they were packed.
>
> Pairs of particles, such as photons or electrons, can be prepared with a similarly tight link. The link is called **entanglement**. One difference turns out to matter more than everything else in this lesson. The two boxes can be described one at a time, each holding a glove, and nothing is missing from those two descriptions. An entangled pair has no such split. There is a single joint description of the pair, and it cannot be taken apart into a complete description of particle A plus a complete description of particle B. Before anybody measures, there is no answer sitting inside either particle waiting to be found.
>
> To measure one of these particles you first choose a direction, the way you choose which way to turn a pair of sunglasses. Each measurement on its own gives a random answer, like a coin flip. The link shows up in the comparison. In a perfect experiment: choose the same direction on both sides and your answers always come out opposite; choose directions at right angles and the two answers are unrelated, like two separate coin flips. As you turn one detector, the agreement slides smoothly from one extreme to the other, and it depends only on the angle between the two chosen directions.
>
> So far nothing rules out the gloves story. Nothing yet stops you saying each particle carried a private list of how it would answer every possible question, with the two lists arranged to fit each other. That idea is what the next part kills.
>
> ### 2. Why the answers cannot have been decided in advance
>
> Suppose each particle really does carry such a list, written before the measurements, of how it would respond in every direction. Then we can work out on paper the largest amount of agreement the two sides could ever show, added up over several different combinations of directions. That limit is a number: **2**.
>
> Now run the experiment. Quantum mechanics predicts the number should reach **2.8** (exactly 2√2). The real measurements come out above 2; the first loophole-free test, in 2015, reported 2.42, and better setups get closer to the 2.8 that quantum mechanics allows. The limit of 2 is broken. No list of pre-set answers, however long or clever, reproduces what the particles actually do. The result is **Bell's theorem**, and the experiment is a **Bell test**.
>
> A conclusion like this is only as strong as what it assumes, so here is the fine print. It holds if three ordinary claims are true. First, nothing that happens on one side affects what happens on the other. Second, each measurement produces one outcome rather than many. Third, the direction chosen on each side is a free choice, not itself fixed in advance in step with the particles. Each of the three has been defended, attacked, or given up by someone. The experiments bear hardest on the first and third. Since 2015 the detectors have sat far enough apart, and switched fast enough, that nothing travelling at light speed could pass between them during a measurement, and enough particles have been caught to make the measured pairs a fair sample of all of them. Later tests let ancient starlight and distant quasars choose the directions, so those choices were settled billions of years before the experiment. The second claim, that each measurement has a single outcome, is where accounts of quantum mechanics part company.
>
> Notice what does not follow. You cannot use the link to send a message. Your own list of results looks like coin flips no matter what your partner does or which direction your partner chooses. The link appears only when the two lists are brought to one place and compared, and that trip happens at ordinary speeds. This is **no-signalling**, and it is why entanglement has never let anyone beat the speed of light.
>
> ### 3. Delicate, useful, and not fully explained
>
> The link survives only while the pair stays isolated. Let one stray particle of air or light interact with either member, and a record of what it did leaks into the surroundings. The sharp pairing between your two particles is then gone, even though those particles are now tangled into everything around them. This spreading of the information away is called **decoherence**, and it is why these experiments run in vacuum chambers at temperatures near absolute zero.
>
> The link is also a resource. Quantum computers hold and move their information in pairs like these, in units called **qubits**, and entanglement is one of the ingredients they rely on; by itself it is not enough to guarantee a speed-up. Two people can also share entangled particles and build a secret key, because anyone listening in has to measure, and measuring breaks the link in a way the two of them can detect. The reach keeps growing: dozens of particles entangled in one experiment, in trapped ions, in arrays of cold atoms, and in superconducting circuits; pairs of photons separated by more than 1,200 km by satellite.
>
> What stays unexplained is not the correlations but the ending. Every run gives one outcome and not another, and no experiment says why that one. Accounts of quantum mechanics disagree about what is happening underneath, while all of them reproduce the numbers seen so far.
>
> **If you remember one sentence:** two particles can be linked so that neither one has its own answer, the link appears only when the results are compared by ordinary means, and it is real, delicate, and cannot carry a message on its own.
