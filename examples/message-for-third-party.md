# Example: a message for a non-technical third party

**Source** (operator's notes): We're on Google Workspace; two-step verification is included in our plan at no extra cost. 12 staff. Phishing and passwords leaked from other sites' breaches are the usual way accounts get taken over, and a taken-over mailbox is a common start for invoice fraud. Prompts only on new devices or after sign-out, roughly a few times a month. Approving a push you didn't start is a known bypass. Setup is about 10 minutes per person.

**Invocation:** `/einstein-simplify for Dana, our office manager, who decides whether we do this`. **Purpose:** decide. **Medium:** a message Dana receives.

**Takeaway:** Passwords get stolen in ways that are hard to fully prevent, and a phone check at sign-in means a stolen password alone is not enough.

**New ideas (3):** passwords leak in ways careful people still miss; a second check on the phone makes a stolen password insufficient; the cost is a few taps a month and about two hours of setup.

**Output** (the message, ready to send):

> Hi Dana,
>
> I'd like us to turn on a phone check when people sign in to their work email. It means a stolen password alone no longer gets someone into an account, and it's already included in what we pay.
>
> Passwords get stolen in ways that are hard to fully prevent. Someone reuses a password on a website that later gets hacked, or a convincing fake sign-in page catches them on a busy day. With the password, a thief can read and send email as that person, which is a common start to fake-invoice scams.
>
> With the phone check on, the right password isn't enough. After typing it, the person also approves the sign-in on their phone, so a thief who has the password but not the phone is stuck. One gap: if someone approves a request they didn't start, the thief gets in, so I'll tell everyone to approve only sign-ins they just made themselves.
>
> Day to day, it means a tap on the phone when signing in on a new device or after signing out, roughly a few times a month. Setup takes about ten minutes per person, about two hours for all twelve of us.
>
> Can I go ahead?

**Operator line** (outside the message):
Left out: the product names, Google Workspace and two-step verification, which Dana does not need in order to decide.
Added: the promise that everyone will be told to approve only the sign-ins they just started, which the notes do not carry.

**Why it looks like this:** Dana decides, so the ask opens the message. The picture (a reused password, a fake page) comes from Dana's world rather than from an analogy. The one place the protection fails is stated because Dana's staff could act on it. Nothing is named, because Dana does not need the terms to decide. "Roughly a few times a month" stays roughly, not "at most". The promise is the one thing the notes do not carry, so it is disclosed rather than folded in silently.
